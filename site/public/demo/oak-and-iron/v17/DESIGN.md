# Oak & Iron: Workbench bento

Calm product-launch minimalism with a point of view. The bento is a workbench: every tile is cut from the same stone-grey stock, and each is sized by what it holds rather than by a uniform card grid. The one accent is the orange of iron at forging heat, and it appears only where heat or urgency belongs: the lead-time tile, flags, the sale code and hover states.

## Colour
- `--bg` #e9e7e3 · `--tile` #f6f5f2 · `--tile-2` #dcd9d3 (art wells) · `--ink` #1f1e1c · `--muted` #5d5a55
- `--heat` #c2410c, with #fff7f2 text on it
- Dark: #141413 ground, #1e1d1b tiles, #f2763a heat.

## Type
- Geist 400/500/650. The headline is set tight at -0.045em, up to 5rem.
- Geist Mono only for real data: prices, ratings, lead time and the email label.

## Layout
A six-column dense grid with a 12px gutter and a 22px radius. The hero takes 4x2, beside a 6–8 week heat tile and a tile of oak-grain contour lines. Three feature tiles follow, then the products: the Long Table is a wide 4-column tile, the other pieces are 2-column tiles. A quote tile and a full-width newsletter tile close the grid. On narrow screens it collapses to a single column of tiles.

## Components
Buttons and inputs are pill-shaped. A button is ink by default and turns to heat on hover. Focus is a 2px heat outline. There are no shadows: depth comes from tone alone.
